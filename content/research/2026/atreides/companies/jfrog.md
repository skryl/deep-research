- **What it does:** Software supply chain platform — Artifactory binary/artifact management, security (Xray, advanced security) and ML-model management for enterprise DevOps.

## Business Overview

JFrog's Artifactory is the de facto system of record for software binaries — every build, dependency, container and, increasingly, ML model an enterprise ships flows through it. The company has layered security (software supply chain scanning, curation) and MLOps/model registry capabilities (JFrog ML) on top, positioning itself as the delivery and governance plane for both human- and AI-written software.

Growth has been accelerating, driven by cloud: FY2025 revenue was $531.8M, up 24%, with cloud revenue of $243.3M up 45%. Q1 2026 revenue was $154.0M, up 26%, with cloud up 50% to $78.9M (51% of revenue, crossing half for the first time). Customers with >$1M ARR reached 80 (from 54 a year earlier), trailing net dollar retention was 120%, and Q1 free cash flow was $37.3M — a rare combination of accelerating growth and solid cash generation at this scale.

## Why Atreides Owns It

JFrog is a picks-and-shovels way to be long the explosion of AI-generated code without taking the dev-tools displacement risk head-on. Baker exited GitLab entirely (after building it to a ~$250M position) citing AI coding-agent pressure on seat-based developer tools — but JFrog monetizes artifacts and throughput, not seats. If coding agents multiply the volume of software being built, the number of binaries, dependencies and models that need storing, scanning and governing goes up, not down; JFrog's accelerating cloud growth and >$1M-customer cohort are early evidence. The position was initiated in Q3 2025, the same quarter the software sleeve began rotating away from seat-exposed names, and Atreides added ~37% more shares in Q1 2026 even as it cut software exposure elsewhere — a small but rising-conviction holding (~$47 implied per share, near where it first entered).

## Risks

- **Consumption can cut both ways:** if AI agents also optimize builds and dependencies, artifact volume growth may disappoint the "more code = more JFrog" thesis.
- GitHub (Microsoft) bundles competing artifact and security features into the platform where developers already live.
- Sub-$30M position — easily exited; Atreides' history shows little patience with software names that stop working.
- Israel-concentrated R&D carries geopolitical operational risk.
- Cloud mix shift pressures gross margin relative to the self-hosted base.
