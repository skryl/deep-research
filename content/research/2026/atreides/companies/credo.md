## Business Overview

Credo's core asset is power-efficient SerDes IP, productized as: **HiWire AECs** (copper cables with DSP re-timing at each end — the dominant revenue line), **optical DSPs** for transceivers, **line-card PHYs/chiplets**, and newer PCIe retimer products. The pitch against optics inside the rack is brutal in its simplicity: management claims its "Zero Flap" AECs deliver up to 1,000× better reliability than laser-based optical modules at materially lower power and cost — and in Baker's power-constrained world, every watt not spent on in-rack optics is a watt available for compute.

Fiscal 2026 (ended ~May 2026) was explosive: revenue reached **~$1.3B, more than tripling YoY** (FY2025 was ~$437M), with Q4 FY2026 alone at a record **$437M, +157% YoY** and +7% sequentially. Non-GAAP gross margin was 68.3% in Q4 and 68.1% for the year; non-GAAP operating margin hit 47.8% for FY2026 and net income rose more than 5× to $662M. Growth was overwhelmingly AEC-driven — unit shipments to hyperscalers contributed over 95% of the product revenue increase — across both 100G/lane and emerging 200G/lane deployments, at hyperscalers and neocloud operators alike. Guidance for fiscal 2027 calls for **>80% revenue growth**, including **>$600M from the optical (DSP) portfolio** — a deliberate second act beyond copper.

The structural caveat: the top three customers are reported at roughly **88% of revenue** — among the most concentrated books in semis.

## Why Atreides Owns It

Credo is the newest expression of the connectivity thesis — initiated as a full ~$100M position in Q1 2026 with no prior history in the filings. The logic slots directly into "watts and wafers": as scale-up domains densify (NVL72-class racks and their successors), the number of high-speed links inside and between adjacent racks grows super-linearly, and AECs win the ≤7-meter regime on power, cost, and reliability. Credo is effectively the anti-optics hedge *within* the optics bucket — if hyperscalers keep pushing copper as far as physics allows to save watts, Credo captures what Lumentum/Coherent lose at short reach, while its optical DSP ramp ($600M+ guided in FY2027) means it participates either way. The timing of entry — the quarter before Credo printed +157% growth and guided +80% — alongside simultaneous Q1 2026 entries elsewhere in the book suggests Atreides rotated profits from trimmed winners (Astera in 2025, Lumentum, Ciena) into the bucket's fastest grower. Baker has not, to our knowledge, discussed Credo by name in public; the thesis fit is inferred from the bucket construction (unconfirmed).

## Risks

- **Extreme customer concentration:** top three customers ~88% of revenue; one hyperscaler redesigning its rack topology or dual-sourcing AECs would crater growth.
- **Copper's physical ceiling:** at 400G/lane and beyond, or with longer scale-up reaches, optics (or CPO) may reclaim the in-rack domain AECs currently own.
- **Competition:** Marvell, Broadcom, Astera (Taurus), Spectra7, and point-solution entrants all target AEC/DSP sockets; 68% gross margins are an invitation.
- **Valuation:** the stock more than tripled over 2025–26; at the fund's entry it priced much of the FY2027 +80% guide already.
- **Optical DSP execution:** the $600M FY2027 optical target is a new market against entrenched incumbents (Marvell, Broadcom) — a miss would break the diversification narrative.
