- **HQ:** Cambridge, Massachusetts
- **What it does:** Customer platform (CRM, marketing, sales, service) for small and mid-market businesses, sold on a freemium-to-suite subscription model.

## Business Overview

HubSpot built its franchise on inbound marketing software and has expanded into a multi-hub "customer platform" — Marketing, Sales, Service, Content, Operations and Commerce hubs on a shared CRM — aimed at SMBs and the mid-market. Revenue is almost entirely subscription: of FY2025's $3.13B total (up 19%), $3.06B was subscription.

The AI story is the whole fight here. HubSpot is shipping its **Breeze** family of AI agents and copilots across the platform while trying to hold profitability — non-GAAP operating income reached $581.9M in FY2025 (vs. $460.2M in 2024), and GAAP operating margin crossed into positive territory (0.2%) for the first time. Q1 2026 showed no demand cliff: revenue of $881.0M was up 23% as reported (18% constant currency), with GAAP net income of $32.6M versus a loss a year earlier.

The stock told a different story than the fundamentals through 2025: HUBS derated severely as the market repriced seat-based SMB software as the most exposed category to AI agents, with implied prices in Atreides' own filings falling from ~$468/share (Q3 2025) to ~$244/share (Q1 2026).

## Why Atreides Owns It

HubSpot is arguably the purest public referendum on Baker's SaaS thesis. His frame (Invest Like the Best EP.451, Dec 2025): incumbent SaaS faces a "life or death decision" — sacrifice margin to ship AI agents, or be undercut by lean AI-native startups — and "anything you can verify, you can automate." CRM workflows for SMBs sit squarely in that crosshair, which is why the market took the stock down ~70% from its early-2025 highs even as revenue compounded at ~20%.

Atreides has traded the controversy rather than marrying a side. It bought a $114M position in Q3 2025 (implied ~$468/share) as the AI-fear derating got going, exited entirely in Q4 2025, then re-entered in Q1 2026 at roughly half the price (~$244 implied) with nearly the same share count. The re-entry reads as a valuation call: at mid-$200s, HubSpot is priced as an AI loser, while Breeze gives it a credible path to being a margin-sacrificing survivor in exactly the way Baker says is necessary. The whipsaw also fits the fund's broader Q1 2026 software rotation — out of dev-tools casualties (GitLab), into names where the AI-displacement risk looks overdiscounted.

## Risks

- **The AI bear case may simply be right:** agentic CRM from AI-native startups (or from OpenAI/Microsoft platform layers) could structurally shrink seat counts and pricing in SMB software.
- **SMB customer base** is the most macro-sensitive and churn-prone segment of enterprise software.
- **Margin sacrifice is real:** if Breeze succeeds by Baker's own logic, it does so by trading margin for survival — the FY2025 margin inflection could reverse.
- **Whipsaw risk for the fund:** Atreides has already been wrong-footed once on timing here; a 1.1% position implies conviction is moderate.
- Competition from Salesforce moving down-market with Agentforce pricing pressure.
