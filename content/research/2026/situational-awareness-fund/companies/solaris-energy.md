## Business

Solaris Energy Infrastructure (formerly Solaris Oilfield Infrastructure) operates two segments: a legacy oilfield-logistics business (sand silos, pneumatic systems for fracking) and a fast-growing **mobile distributed-power** business that owns and rents fleets of natural-gas-fired turbine and reciprocating-engine power generators. The power segment was acquired and built out aggressively in 2024–2025, with the customer base expanding from oilfield (gas-on-pad) to data-center (temporary or bridge power for sites awaiting permanent generation).

The 2024 announcements of multi-hundred-MW power-rental contracts with data-center developers — bridge power until permanent generation is permitted and built — moved Solaris from an oilfield-services microcap into a small-cap distributed-power pure play.

## Why It Fits the Thesis

Bloom Energy serves the *permanent* on-site generation need; Solaris serves the *bridge* generation need. AI data-center developers face an awkward gap: the buildings can be ready in 18 months but utility power is 4–7 years away and even Bloom's permanent SOFC takes ~12 months. Solaris's mobile fleet fills that gap with delivered-and-installed gas turbines that can come online in weeks.

## Financial Trajectory

The power segment grew rapidly through 2025; aggregate company revenue has roughly doubled with the segment ramp. Fleet size in the multi-hundred-MW range as of late 2025, with further additions ordered.

## Risks

- **Equipment-rental cyclicality** if AI demand slows.
- **Capex intensity** in scaling the mobile-power fleet.
- **Competition** from larger industrial-rental peers (Aggreko, etc.) that could enter the data-center bridge-power niche.
