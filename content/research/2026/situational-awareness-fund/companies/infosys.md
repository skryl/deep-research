## Business

Infosys is the second-largest Indian IT-services company (behind TCS) and a top global outsourcer of application development, maintenance, business-process services, and digital transformation. Reported FY25 revenue around **$19 B** with operating margins in the low-20s. The customer base is heavily concentrated in US/EU enterprises (financial services, retail, healthcare, telco). The labor model: roughly 320,000+ employees, the majority based in India, billed at substantial markups to onshore equivalents.

The business model has been remarkably stable for two decades: take a US enterprise's "keep-the-lights-on" engineering work, perform it in Bangalore at a fraction of US cost, and capture the spread.

## The Short Thesis

LLM coding agents — Claude Code, Cursor, GitHub Copilot Workspace, Devin and successors, including increasingly the autonomous-agent generation — directly substitute for the activity Infosys sells. The substitution is not gradual:

- **Application maintenance** (the largest Infosys revenue segment): writing tickets, fixing bugs, updating libraries — a workflow LLM agents handle competently *today*.
- **Application development** (rewrite/modernization projects): early adopters report 30–60% throughput gains using LLM coding agents over 2024–2025, with the trend accelerating.
- **Custom-software-development billed by hours**: every hour of human work that an agent can replace at $0.20 of API cost compresses Infosys's revenue line.

The macro pattern is asymmetric: LLM productivity gains accrue first to the *buyer* of labor (the enterprise client), not to the *seller* (Infosys). Indian IT-services firms reportedly began using Copilot internally in 2023–2024 to maintain margins, but the same gains are visible to clients who can renegotiate billing rates downward — squeezing the spread.

The fund's short is structural, not cyclical: it bets that 2026–2028 is the period in which the labor-arbitrage IT-services model erodes from the top of the contract pipeline.

## Why Puts Specifically

- Multi-year contract structure means revenue erosion is *slow* — short-volatility / time-decay-friendly options strategies fit.
- Listed-options liquidity in the INFY ADR is deep enough for the size.
- Disclosure visibility — appears in the 13F as the only thematic short, sending a clear signal about the fund's view.

## Risks (to the Short)

- **Indian IT may successfully reposition** as the labor force that *deploys and operates* LLM-augmented enterprise systems — a different, possibly higher-margin business.
- **Currency tailwinds** (INR weakness) cushion margins.
- **Reported quarterly results have remained reasonable** through 2024–2025 — the secular thesis is forward-looking and not yet visible in trailing financials.
- **Squeeze risk** — concentrated single-stock put position is sensitive to short-term rallies.
