## Business

Sandisk is the **NAND flash and SSD pure-play** spun out of Western Digital in February 2025. The company holds a long-standing 50/50 fab joint venture with Kioxia (the former Toshiba Memory) operating multiple 3D NAND fabs in Japan, plus its own controller/firmware engineering and a strong consumer brand (SanDisk-branded SSDs, memory cards).

Following the spin, SNDK is a clean exposure to:

- **Enterprise SSDs** (data-center capacity drives, high-performance NVMe drives)
- **Client SSDs** (PC and laptop)
- **Consumer storage** (cards, USB)
- **Mobile NAND** (UFS for smartphones)

Western Digital retained the HDD business, completing a separation that had been logical for years.

## Why It Fits the Thesis

Two AI-relevant pressures favor NAND structurally:

1. **Inference-time KV-cache offload to fast NAND** as long-context inference scales — a meaningful new demand source.
2. **Training dataset persistence at multi-PB scale** drives enterprise NVMe demand from labs.

Combined with cyclical pricing recovery from the 2023–2024 down-cycle and surviving suppliers' capex discipline, NAND ASPs and Sandisk margins should compound through 2026–2027. The fund's choice of SNDK over Micron (which it was actually short via puts in Q3 2025) reflects a view that **NAND is the better-positioned memory class** vs. DRAM/HBM, where consensus is already crowded.

## Financial Trajectory

The standalone Sandisk reports a clean NAND-only P&L since the February 2025 spin. Revenue is subject to the standard memory cyclicality but the trajectory through 2025 was strongly positive on price recovery.

## Risks

- **NAND pricing volatility** — commodity exposure remains.
- **Kioxia JV dependency** for fab capacity and capex decisions.
- **HBM cannibalization** if AI inference architectures shift more aggressively to DRAM-based caching.
